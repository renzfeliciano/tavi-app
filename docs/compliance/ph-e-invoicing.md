## 1. Invoices under the EOPT Act (RR 7-2024) — in force now

- The **invoice** is the primary evidence of a sale of goods *and* services; official receipts are no longer required and may be used only as supplementary documents (S1, Sec. 6 A.4).
- An invoice must be issued for each sale of **₱500 or more** (adjusted for inflation every three years); **VAT-registered sellers issue one for every sale regardless of amount** (S1, Sec. 6 A.1–A.2; Sec. 3 A.1).
- Only a **duly registered** invoice counts: printed with an Authority to Print, or generated by a system registered with the BIR (S1, Sec. 6 A.1, B.19–B.21; Sec. 7).
- **Required contents** (S1, Sec. 6 B): seller's registered name (optionally with trade name); "VAT Reg TIN" or "Non-VAT Reg TIN" with the TIN and branch code; registered business address; the word "Invoice" (e.g. "Service Invoice"); date; space for the buyer's registered name, address and TIN (not required for business-to-consumer sales); serial number printed prominently; quantity; unit cost; description; total (VAT included, if VAT-registered); VAT shown separately; for mixed sales, VATable Sales / VAT Amount / Zero-Rated Sales / VAT-Exempt Sales; "EXEMPT" for sellers not subject to VAT or percentage tax; Senior Citizen / PWD / NAAC / Solo Parent / MOV sales: ID number, the 5% or 20% discount and 12% VAT-exemption breakdown, signature.
- For sales of **₱1,000 or more to a VAT-registered buyer**, the buyer's registered name, address and TIN (S1, Sec. 3 B.4).
- **System-generated invoices** (CAS, CBA or similar) print at the bottom the **Permit to Use number or Acknowledgement Certificate Control Number, the series range and the date issued**, and "REPRINT" on reprints (S1, Sec. 6 B.21).
- **Supplementary documents** (billing statements, statements of account, delivery receipts, acknowledgement or payment receipts, credit/debit memos and similar) must show **"THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT VALID FOR CLAIM OF INPUT TAX."** in bold (S1, Sec. 2 item 2; Sec. 6 B.15).

## 2. Electronic invoicing (RR 11-2025, RR 26-2025, RMC 98-2026)

### Who must issue electronic invoices by 31 December 2026

Per S3, S4 (Sec. III, IV.1) and S5:

1. Taxpayers engaged in **e-commerce or internet transactions** classified as Small, Medium or Large (**Micro taxpayers exempt**);
2. Taxpayers under the **Large Taxpayers Service**;
3. **Large taxpayers** under the EOPT Act and RR 8-2024;
4. Taxpayers using **CAS or CBA with accounting records (with electronic invoicing), and other invoicing software**;
5. Others the Commissioner may require.

Later, once the BIR's system is ready and by separate regulations: exporters, registered business enterprises with tax incentives, and POS users (S2, S3).

**Micro taxpayers are not covered** by the mandate (S4 IV.1, S5). They may adopt e-invoicing voluntarily (S2; S4 IV.9, with a Permit to Issue). Without e-invoices, a micro taxpayer issues a registered manual invoice or may use CAS, CRM or POS (S2).

**E-commerce** is defined broadly and explicitly includes "sale of creative or professional services, on-demand or freelance services or digital services supplied over the internet" and "home services (like cleaning or repairs)" requested on demand over the internet (S2).

**Taxpayer size** (S6): Micro under ₱3,000,000 gross sales; Small ₱3M to under ₱20M; Medium ₱20M to under ₱1B; Large ₱1B and up (gross sales net of VAT).

### What counts as an electronic invoice (S4 IV.4–IV.7)

All three must hold:

- generated by a **duly registered, approved or accredited** accounting/invoicing software or system **in a structured electronic format**;
- **delivered to the buyer electronically**: email, online viewing, QR code, mobile app, web platform or similar;
- its data can be **electronically extracted, processed and transmitted to the BIR** for sales reporting.

Invoices made in Word, Excel, Google Docs/Sheets or similar are **not** valid e-invoices (S4 IV.4). A system-generated invoice that is printed, from a system that can't issue electronically and report sales data, is **not** an e-invoice; it follows the non-electronic invoice rules (S2; S4 IV.5).

A printed copy of an e-invoice may be given for reference, including in B2C sales where electronic delivery isn't practical, provided the invoice was generated and issuable electronically (S4 IV.7). The seller must give a printed copy on request (S4 IV.4).

### Format and data (S4 IV.6)

- The BIR's EIS uses **JSON** for transmitting sales data. Systems may use other structured formats internally if they can convert to the BIR's format.
- The JSON data comes mainly from the minimum invoice contents (Tax Code Sec. 113 and RR 7-2024), **plus details such as discounts and withholding taxes**. The technical specifications are on the EIS Certification Portal, `eis-cert.bir.gov.ph` (S4 IV.15). The exact field list isn't in the circular itself.

### Corrections (S4 IV.8)

- **"An issued electronic invoice shall not be deleted, altered, or modified."**
- A **decrease** is made with a duly authorized **credit note/memo** referencing the original. An **increase** is made with a **new electronic invoice**. Detailed sales-adjustment rules will come in a separate issuance.

### Registration and certification (S4 IV.12–IV.15)

- **Permit to Issue (PTI) Electronic Invoice**: required *before* issuing. It's filed with the taxpayer's RDO or LT Office, evaluated within **20 working days** of complete documents, and specific to the approved software/system. It is **distinct from the PTU/Acknowledgement Certificate** for CAS.
- Changing, migrating to or replacing the invoicing software needs a new or amended PTI. Head office and branches share one PTI number; different software per branch means separate PTIs.
- **EIS Certification**: online testing of the taxpayer's system, with **5 mandatory tests (7 with API callback)** (S4 II.6). It must be obtained **within 6 months of the PTI**, or the PTI may be revoked.
- **Permit to Transmit (PTT)**: only once the Commissioner activates electronic sales reporting.

### Options for taxpayers (S4 IV.3, S5)

An **in-house** solution, a **commercially acquired** solution, or an **Electronic Invoicing Service Provider (ESP)**. An ESP is a juridical entity organized or licensed in the Philippines. It provides e-invoicing, sales reporting, integration, validation, transmission, storage, preservation, retrieval, reconciliation, migration and audit-support services. It keeps an authorized representative and accountable technical, compliance, data-protection and security officers in the Philippines (S4 II.5). **ESP rules will come in a separate issuance**; the BIR said "within the month" on 23 Sep 2026 (S5).

### Electronic sales reporting (S4 IV.2, S5)

It is **separate** from e-invoicing and applies only after the BIR issues its implementing rules. The BIR's instruction: "focus on complying with the electronic invoicing rules" for now.

### Downtime (S4 IV.11)

If the system can't issue, the taxpayer issues a **BIR-authorized manual invoice**. After restoration, every manual invoice is replaced by an e-invoice bearing the manual invoice's reference number.

### Incentive (S2)

Taxpayers setting up electronic sales reporting may deduct an **additional 100% (micro and small) or 50% (medium and large)** of the setup cost from taxable income, once, in the year it's completed.

## 3. What this means for TAVI (analysis, to confirm)

1. **Most early TAVI users may be micro taxpayers** (under ₱3M gross sales) and therefore **not required** to e-invoice. They still need *registered* invoices. That means either a BIR-printed booklet, with TAVI producing billing statements and quotations (supplementary documents with the notice), or registering TAVI as their system (AC/PTU) so TAVI prints their invoices (D13).
2. **Small and larger users who invoice through TAVI would likely be covered**, under "other invoicing software", and possibly as "e-commerce" for services sold over the internet. They would need a PTI and EIS certification, and TAVI would need to produce structured JSON per the BIR's specification. **TAVI does not do this yet**, and the ESP rules are still pending.
3. **Editing issued invoices conflicts with the e-invoice rules.** Decision D7 lets a sent invoice be edited until the first payment, but S4 IV.8 forbids altering an issued e-invoice (credit note to decrease, new invoice to increase). **Proposal for the founder:** once a business is in invoice mode (registered invoices), issued invoices become immutable in TAVI. Corrections go through void-and-reissue now and credit notes later. D7 would stay only for billing statements.
4. **Structured data from day one.** TAVI already stores every invoice field as structured data (integer amounts, per-line tax snapshots, discounts), and payments will record withholding. That is the data the JSON requires (S4 IV.6.b), so a later JSON export is an addition, not a rebuild.
5. **Downtime fallback** (S4 IV.11) implies TAVI will eventually need a way to record manual invoice numbers and link replacement e-invoices to them.

## 4. Questions for the RDO and a CPA

1. Our users are small service businesses. If a **micro** taxpayer uses TAVI (a web app) to issue its invoices, must it register TAVI as a **CAS** (AC/PTU), or is another registration route expected for cloud invoicing software?
2. For a **small** taxpayer (₱3M–₱20M) using TAVI: does "other invoicing software" in RMC 98-2026 Sec. III.4 cover a subscription web app like TAVI, making e-invoicing mandatory by 31 Dec 2026?
3. Does a service business that takes orders or sends quotes and invoices **online** fall under "e-commerce or internet transactions" (RR 11-2025's definition includes professional and freelance services supplied over the internet)?
4. For a multi-tenant SaaS used by many taxpayers: is **EIS certification per taxpayer**, or can the software be certified once? Should TAVI plan to become an **ESP** once those rules are issued?
5. Are **quotations** "supplementary documents" that must carry "THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT VALID FOR CLAIM OF INPUT TAX."?
6. May a **non-electronic** registered invoice (CAS-generated, printed) be corrected after issuance, or is void-and-reissue / credit memo the only correct method?
7. Where is the **JSON field specification** published, and does TAVI need a BIR account on `eis-cert.bir.gov.ph` to read it?
8. When a corporate customer **withholds creditable tax** (BIR Form 2307), is it enough to record the withheld amount against the invoice, or must the invoice show it?
